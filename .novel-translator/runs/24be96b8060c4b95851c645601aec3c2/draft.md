**Chapter 3**

**Episode 27: One Rainy Day After School, Part 1**

"Tsutomu-kun, we're closed today."

Tsutomu saw that message right after the chime marking the end of the last class of the week rang out on Friday afternoon.

Usually, this was the time when students would shrug off the teachers' protests and launch themselves toward the weekend—but today, every corner of the school was wrapped in a heavy, oppressive atmosphere.

In that classroom, he tapped the smartphone he'd pulled from his pocket. He'd noticed the incoming message during class.

What appeared was a notice that today's shift was cancelled. It had been sent by the owner of the shop where Tsutomu worked part-time.

—Well, nothing to be done about that, I suppose.

He sighed lightly and put his phone away. He adjusted his glasses.

Whether it was his imagination or not, his head was beginning to ache with a tight, constricting pain.

He turned his gaze toward the window—outside, it was a torrential downpour.

A proper "can't see an inch ahead" level of chaos.

He stared for a while at the sky covered in black clouds beyond the curtain of water, then let out another long breath.

As one of the students who had to make it home in this, he couldn't help but feel gloomy.

★

The first-semester midterms were over, and the calendar had entered June.

In Japan, this season edging toward summer was, above all, rain, rain, and more rain.

The so-called tsuyu—the rainy season—and today was no exception. Between the humidity and everything else, the discomfort index was off the charts.

On top of that, an unseasonable typhoon that had formed over the Pacific was moving north, stirring up the tsuyu front, yadda yadda yadda.

At any rate, heavy rain was expected, and Twitter had been buried in the topic since morning.

"I don't wanna work, forsooth."

"Days like this should be automatic days off."

"Rain or snow or a plague sweeping through, Japan never changes."

"I don't think I can make it home today..."

"Isn't that the usual case?"

The students who'd finished their classes all wore dull, gloomy eyes fixed on the windows.

None of them boarded at school. They had to go home. They wanted to go home. But—

If the weather forecast could be trusted, conditions were only going to deteriorate from here.

"Ugh, this is unbearable."

"You said it."

The one who sighed while clapping a hand on Tsutomu's shoulder was one of his few friends, Shiro Amakusa.

A playboy with dyed-brown hair and a sweet face—and the ringmaster of the so-called "Gariben Notes," the study notes Tsutomu had created.

He was a friendly guy with a wide social circle, and for a man who always wore a flippant expression, it was rare to see his face shadowed like this.

Not that Tsutomu could blame him. He was probably wearing a similar expression himself.

"And it's the weekend, of all weekends. Can't do a damn thing."

"Why don't you stay home and study?"

"Ah-ah-ah, don't wanna hear your sensible talk."

Shiro covered both ears and wriggled his body theatrically.

To put it mildly, it was annoying, so Tsutomu looked away.

The weather was already enough to make anyone want to hole up; he didn't need any extra nonsense in his field of vision.

"Working today, Tsutomu-san?"

"No, I've got the day off."

When he showed Shiro the message from the shop owner, Shiro nodded. "Yeah, figured."

Even if they kept the shop open against the combined assault of tsuyu and typhoon, customers were unlikely to show up. It would just burn money on wasted overhead—and not just for customers, either. The other staff would have a hell of a time commuting back and forth between the shop and home.

As someone on the payroll, he was grateful the owner had made the call to close up quickly.

"No point dawdling here. Let's head home before the trains stop."

"Probably for the best."

Tsutomu's reply was oddly detached, as if it concerned someone else.

"Must be nice for the kids who live right by school."

Shiro shrugged, and Tsutomu nodded silently.

For students commuting from far away, securing transportation was a critical issue. Looking at the current sky and the way the clouds were moving—not that they were exactly moving—there was a real chance the trains would be suspended.

Even the classmates who usually wallowed in weekend freedom and hung around after school seemed to be heading straight home today, at least.

"See you, Tsutomu. Next week."

"Yeah, see you."

He watched his friend's back as Shiro waved and left the classroom, then cracked his neck.

Shiro wasn't wrong. Tsutomu lived alone in an apartment not far from the school.

Having a place near school made everything more convenient. Since entering high school, he'd come to appreciate that deeply. It was a genuine blessing.

With weather this terrible, even with an umbrella, there was no avoiding getting drenched—but simply getting home wasn't a problem.

As long as he made it back, he could take a bath and change. He had food stocked up, so dinner was manageable. Bath, dinner—all of it was easy when you lived alone.

—I wonder how Tachibana's doing?

The thought crossed his mind.

He didn't say it aloud.

Tachibana.

"Matsurika Tachibana."

A girl his age, in the same class.

The school's most beautiful girl, who'd won the miss contest at last year's school festival.

And a girl who carried another face as "RIKA"—the erotic selfie secret account that Tsutomu rooted for.

After various twists and turns, she'd recently asked him to "be friends," and he'd nodded. They'd already exchanged social media IDs.

She was just over 160 centimeters tall. Her weight was top secret.

Her lustrous straight black hair reached her waist. From her silky, glossy mane, you could tell she'd never once missed her daily care routine.

Large black eyes, a cleanly defined nose bridge, and small lips gleaming with a pink sheen.

The features packed into her head—slightly small in proportion to her body—were arranged by the gods themselves.

Her chest swelled generously from beneath her thin summer clothes, tapered through a sharply cinched waist, and continued into her lower half. The beauty of that curve could only be called perfect.

Her rear was set high, and the white legs extending from her short skirt—hemmed up to just barely within dress-code bounds—were slender yet healthily fleshed in a way that was irresistible.

She embodied beauty and eros pushed to the absolute limits of an adolescent boy's ideals and fantasies. Her level of perfection was hard to believe she was a real person.

And the girl called "Matsurika Tachibana" didn't stop there. On top of her perfect looks, she had excellent athletic ability and a sharp mind.

Her communication skills were superb as well, and she was one of the central figures in the classroom. It was fair to call her something like the sun that illuminated the class.

But—she was also someone who fell in love easily. While she received passionate support from men, judging from recent exchanges, it seemed she drew complicated feelings from other women more than a little.

When she'd tried to ask Tsutomu to teach her, she'd been mindful of being seen. It seemed she put extraordinary effort into maintaining her charismatic image.

Lately, when Tsutomu let his guard down, his eyes would end up following her. He didn't question it.

The other boys were much the same. It wasn't as if Tsutomu was particularly special.

Tachibana was a girl who drew the eye, after all. She captured male attention with particular force.

It had always been that way, and it still was now.

—Friends... huh.

At the afterparty for the first-semester midterms, she'd told him directly that she wanted to be friends. Of course, his answer was yes.

Several days had passed since he'd formally (?) become friends with Tachibana. If anyone asked whether anything had changed—no, nothing in particular had.

She still shone brilliantly at the center of the classroom, but Tsutomu never actively approached her.

Thanks to the incident surrounding the "Gariben Notes"—one of the things connecting the two of them—Tsutomu had been treated as a dangerous person by his classmates, or so it felt.

He'd never been particularly close to the rest of the class to begin with, and he didn't feel the need to fawn over them or match their conversations, so Tsutomu remained mostly alone, as always. Shiro was about the only exception.

"What does it even mean to be friends?"

He'd once asked Shiro that, without mentioning that the person in question was Tachibana. It was exposed immediately.

His flippant friend flashed an irritating, snickering grin and said:

"You and Tachibana-san don't really give off a friends vibe, though."

"Oh, is that so?"

"Yeah. I mean, she's got a ton of friends, but you don't come across as one of them, you know?"

Shiro—friendly, and sharp when it came to the nuances of human relationships—had a take that really struck home.

But that raised a new question. Namely:

"Then what kind of relationship do Tachibana and I have?"

Since he'd been found out anyway, he stopped trying to hide it.

He wanted to hear what Shiro honestly thought.

"Tsutomu-san, what's the point in asking me that?"

Shiro wore a wry smile, a rare sight for him.

"What do you mean?"

"The relationship between you two is something for you two to decide. Right?"

It was a perfectly irrefutable argument.

Since then, Tsutomu had been thinking about it constantly.

The answer—still hadn't come.

—Did Tachibana... go home already?

He looked around the classroom, which had grown noticeably emptier, but he couldn't catch sight of the person he was looking for.

For better or worse, she was a conspicuous girl. There was no way he'd mistake her, or miss her.

"Matsurika Tachibana" was not in the classroom. That was certain.

Half relieved, half disappointed. Tsutomu was at a loss over a complicated emotion he still didn't quite recognize.
