**Chapter 34: A Peaceful After-Dinner Moment at First Glance**

The sound of running water. The clatter of dishes.
The sound of the clock's hour hand marking time. A cheerful hum.
The roar of the storm intensifying outside.

Each individual sound was perfectly ordinary, yet when all combined, they made Tsutomu feel deeply unsettled.
After finishing dinner with Matsurika, he had remained on the living room sofa, brooding and killing time.
It wasn't that he was too exhausted to do anything—though he couldn't deny feeling that way a little, given everything that had happened today.
He had actually tried to get up to clean up. The longer you put off dealing with dirty dishes, the more tedious it becomes.
That was one of the precious lessons he'd learned in his sixteen-odd years of life. But then—a voice called out, "Wait."

*"I can't just eat a meal you made and do nothing in return."*

He couldn't stop Matsurika from carrying the dishes to the kitchen.
Struck by the combined assault of the rainy season and a typhoon, he'd offered to walk her to the station.
When they arrived, the trains were suspended, so he'd offered to let her stay the night.
She'd taken a bath to warm up, filled her stomach with Tsutomu's homemade dinner.
Having been taken care of all this way, Matsurika rolled up her sleeves, seizing the moment.

"You're a guest today, Tachibana. There's really no need to worry about it."

"Nope. I've already owed Kariya-kun way too many favors."

"Isn't that a different matter entirely?"

"It's not. You go rest, Kariya-kun."

When she put it that way, he couldn't very well wrestle the work away from her.
And so, he left the after-dinner dishes to Matsurika, which brought them to the present.

*—What is this... what?*

Being able to relax after a meal was a blessing.
Having someone else clean up in his place was wonderful.
Having lived alone for about a year, he'd grown used to doing everything by himself... so this kind of rest was genuinely refreshing.
In fact, having anyone other than himself in this house was a first.

He turned his gaze from the living room toward the kitchen.
Through his glasses, he could see Matsurika washing the dishes.
Her waist-length straight black hair was tied into a ponytail at the back of her head—a fresh look. She was wearing nothing but the white dress shirt Tsutomu had lent her. *Nothing but the white dress shirt* (this part is important!).
The class idol, with whom he'd had virtually no real contact until recently, was doing housework in his kitchen—the sight was almost blindingly vivid.

"Something wrong, Kariya-kun?"

"...No, I was just moved seeing Tachibana standing in my kitchen."

"Oh, I see."

He'd been staring, so she called out to him, and he answered honestly.
Matsurika's reaction was curt, but the edge in her voice was slightly lifted.
She continued washing the dishes, humming a tune. Her hands moved with confidence.

*—She's pretty experienced at this...*

He found it surprising, but voicing that casually might sour her mood, so he kept quiet.
He'd already been told that she was sensitive to how people looked at her.
There were only two people in this room right now—Tsutomu and Matsurika—so hiding or deflecting wasn't an option.
In that case, he might as well look openly. And so, he'd been sitting with his arms crossed, watching her.

"..."

"..."

"Hey, Kariya-kun."

"What?"

"It's kind of hard to concentrate when you stare at me like that."

"Understood. What a shame."

Matsurika in just a dress shirt was something he could look at forever without getting bored.
It really, truly was a shame. If she was uncomfortable, he had no choice but to give up.
Apparently sensing his feelings from his tone, Matsurika sighed and made a suggestion.

"If you've got nothing to do, why not watch TV or something?"

"TV, huh..."

He glanced at the black flat-screen television, its power off.
The remote was sitting on the table.
All he had to do was reach out a little, but he didn't feel like it.
Watching TV in this situation felt like a waste.

"Well, I'm the one who suggested it, but I can't imagine Kariya-kun watching TV."

"Probably not. I almost never watch it."

"Don't you mean you *never* watch it?"

"How rude. I do watch the news and weather forecast."

Tsutomu generally had little interest in TV.
In this day and age, if he wanted to look something up, a computer or smartphone sufficed.
However, relying on the internet for information tended to filter out things he didn't actively care about.
To avoid being too out of touch with the world, it was better to leave the TV running during free time.
...His stepfather had taught him that, so he made a point of practicing it. While convincing himself that current events would appear on university entrance exams.
He couldn't actually tell whether it was working.

"Okay, okay. Anything else?"

"...Music shows, occasionally."

"Wait, seriously? Kariya-kun watches music shows? That's shocking..."

Matsurika's eyes went wide.
Her voice went up an octave.
She seemed genuinely surprised, which made him involuntarily pull a sullen face.

"Well, I can't deny that's how you'd see me. It's not like I'm glued to the TV or anything to be proud of."

Tsutomu never really had much free time to begin with.
School, part-time job, housework, and then time for previewing and reviewing lessons.
The noise from the TV would interfere with his studies. So he turned it off.
Despite his stepfather's advice, the TV's time in the spotlight naturally kept shrinking.

"Hmm... TV, huh."

Might as well, he thought, picking up the remote and turning on the TV.
A new sound joined the room. It felt noisy.

"Kariya-kun, want some coffee after your meal?"

"I'd appreciate that. Wait, do you know where it is?"

"It's probably the same in every house, isn't it?"

Matsurika's exasperated voice.
She rummaged around the kitchen, filled the kettle with water, and put it on the stove.
She easily found the instant coffee and sugar, then asked,

"How much sugar?"

"None."

"Milk?"

"No. Black is fine."

"Ohh, so Kariya-kun drinks it black."

"Yeah."

"You're just lazy, aren't you."

"..."

He didn't like being unilaterally judged like that, but her assessment hit the mark.
Like when he drank cocoa at school, it wasn't that Tsutomu disliked sweet things.
He drank his coffee black at home simply because it was less hassle.
Besides, he mostly drank barley tea at home, so he didn't really drink coffee much at all.
Of course, that was also because preparing it was a hassle. He did occasionally drink packaged iced coffee, though he was out of it today.
Living alone meant cutting unnecessary steps wherever possible—that was his philosophy, but he doubted that logic would get through to Matsurika, so he stayed silent.

Unable to keep looking at Matsurika's smirking face, he changed the channel on the TV.
First came the weather forecast, then an anchor reporting in the downpour, then a press conference with some meteorological agency bigwig.
Every channel was dominated by the rain front and typhoon covering all of Japan. Nothing particularly caught his eye.

"Anything interesting on?"

Matsurika had returned from the kitchen without his noticing, setting down two cups of hot coffee on the table before sitting down beside him.
With a casual motion, she undid her ponytail, and her long, straight black hair cascaded down beautifully.
She gave a light shake of her head, and her hair rippled in a neat wave. A sweet, warm scent brushed past Tsutomu's nose.
Alone on the sofa in his own home with the class idol. And in just a dress shirt with bare legs, no less.
The situation was so naturally formed yet so unnatural that his heart began to thrum irregularly.

"No, nothing. Apparently the weather won't clear up until tomorrow."

His voice came out slightly stiff—probably from being flustered by her sudden closeness.
If he could notice it himself, it was very likely Matsurika picked up on it too.
He thought he might be overthinking it, but he couldn't help feeling awkward.

"Hmm, so the trains are out too?"

"Seems that way."

The image on TV showed a massive terminal station in the metropolitan area, similar to what they'd actually seen at their local station.
He looked outside—as expected, pitch black. The howling rain and wind stirred a primal fear.
They'd taken a roundabout route and gotten soaked, but at least they'd made it back safely. He felt a belated wave of relief.
Compared to the raging storm outside... the two of them were here, alone in a small, peaceful space.
No—he couldn't quite decide whether this situation could truly be called peaceful.
The emotional state of Tsutomu, the occupant of this room, had been in freefall since a moment ago.
While Tsutomu's heart raced, Matsurika sitting beside him let out a languid sigh. Her cheeks puffed slightly, her jet-black eyes fixed on the TV screen.
He couldn't help but wish that nothing else would happen.
Their night together had only just begun.
